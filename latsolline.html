Conclusion & Reflection

What We Learned Today

  • Line Segment — has 2 endpoints, measurable length (segment AB = segment BA).
  • Ray — has 1 endpoint, extends infinitely in one direction (Ray PQ ≠ Ray QP).
  • Line — extends infinitely in both directions, cannot be measured.
  • Parallel lines (//) — same plane, constant distance, never meet.
  • Perpendicular lines (⊥) — intersect at exactly 90°.
  • Coincident lines — lie on top of each other, share infinitely many points.
  • Intersecting lines — meet at one point (perpendicular is a special case).

Reflection Questions

Take a moment to think about these questions:

  • Which type of line relationship was easiest for you to identify? Why?
  • Which question challenged you the most? What made it tricky?
  • Can you find 3 examples of parallel lines and 3 examples of perpendicular lines in your classroom right now?
  • Why do you think train tracks are designed to be parallel? What would happen if they weren't?
  • How does understanding lines help architects and engineers build safe structures?

Key Takeaways

Geometry is everywhere around us — in letters, buildings, nature, and even in the clock on the wall! Understanding how lines relate to each other helps us make sense of the world and solve real-world problems.
